# Experimental major river outlets

In New Game, open **Advanced → Terrain / Hydrology** and enable **Termination valley carving**. **Enable major-outlet breaching (experimental)** starts off. It requires Visible terrain and zero TIN filtering. The extra pass uses the completed terrain after infill and ordinary carving, with the same retained ocean mask.

The defaults are a maximum cut of 0.01 planet units, a combined excavation volume of 0.000002 times the cubed base radius, and a maximum new channel length of 20 degrees along the surface. The existing minimum carving runoff and carve width also apply. The depth limit is measured from the post-carving surface. At most 12 closed catchments with extracted river networks are attempted in descending independent runoff order.

The planner starts with a graph of exact spill witnesses and shared edges between facets with observed downstream destinations. It ranks up to three corridors to the primary ocean by estimated excavation volume, peak barrier, length, protected geometry, and the independent runoff of the basins crossed. Terrain search is confined to the selected basin strips and local bands around the receiving drainage. Each route must reach an uncut receiver with its own primary-ocean trace.

Refinement works backward from possible exits to bound the remaining length and the lowest bed that could still reach an exit. It rejects cyclic and dominated geometry-equivalent prefixes while retaining distinct cuts that could blend differently or produce different TIN flow. The planner blends banks using the valley carving profile and measures excavation on the projected terrain. The TIN must trace the proposed floor and former network sources to the primary ocean on that mesh. Existing retained shoreline facets stay protected, and lake geometry and ownership must agree before and after the proposed edit.

If the rebuilt TIN takes a downhill diversion and fails verification, the planner keeps the traced prefix and recomputes the highest usable bed at every remaining station. It combines the minimum downhill grade with slope constraints from adjacent edges and the steepest facet direction. It also constrains the observed departure between stations. A regrade propagates through the remaining saddles to the uncut receiver. The banks are blended again before remeasuring excavation and retracing. A new adaptive grade is capped at 1:1, with an allowance to retain a naturally steeper grade.

Each corridor gets at most four revisions, and a batch gets at most seven verification TIN builds. A repeated diversion and destination stop further revisions of that corridor. The selected receiver must still pass its own check, and the final batch must pass the existing source, lake, shoreline, and ocean checks before it can change the terrain. Rejection can mean the bounded search or profile revision found no feasible route. A blocked-edge depth describes the smallest over-budget edge observed during search. A completed profile's required depth describes its route. Basins that require water storage above their floor remain closed in this experiment.

## Diagnosing a rejected attempt

Enter the floor vertex in **TIN Basin Live Inspector** and select **Inspect outlet attempt for basin ID**. Orange shows the initial profile. Blue shows the last tested revision where the profile changed. Both are lifted above the live surface to show relative bed changes. Purple shows actual drainage on the last verification TIN, cyan shows live drainage, and green shows the original receiver. Magenta marks the remaining divergence or verification failure. Yellow ticks mark search frontier vertices, and the yellow path shows the closest receiver hint. The report gives exact facet coordinates and radii on the pre-edit and proposed TINs.

`GeodesicMajorOutletIteration` reports the selected corridor and each tested trace's first unrecovered departure, competing and intended slopes, changed bed elevations, corrected junction count, projected excavation, terminal, and outcome. It flags a downstream move or repeated drainage state. `GeodesicMajorOutletPonding` reports geometric storage to the first spill where the existing basin or the rejected trace's terminal has a spill witness.

`GeodesicMajorOutletCorridors` reports the estimated costs and basin sequences of the coarse corridors. The selected corridor and actual terrain route can cross different basin boundaries within the refinement region. `GeodesicMajorOutletSearch` reports admitted and expanded labels, unique expanded vertices, peak queued labels, retired labels, remaining frontier, receiver probes, completed profiles, and prune counts. Explored basin IDs come from the observed closed destinations of facets incident to expanded vertices.

The fixed terrain bounds are 8,192 admitted labels, 512 uncached receiver probes, and 24 full profile evaluations per attempt, shared across the selected corridors. The coarse search is capped at 512 expansions. `search-budget` means a refinement budget stopped exploration before a feasible profile was selected. `FrontierExhausted` applies to the selected corridor regions. `no-coarse-primary-corridor` means the observed coarse graph could not connect the source to the primary ocean. Neither outcome proves that every possible route through the terrain is infeasible.

`closestReceiverHintHops=0` means the explored vertex is tagged by a nearby primary-ocean centroid trace or is a classified primary-ocean vertex. `closestReceiverHintEligibility` identifies the next check for that label. A hint can be the source itself, require incision at the entry, lie outside the chosen exit band, or fail its exact trace's protected-water, retained-boundary, ocean-component, or downhill-grade check.

`GeodesicMajorOutletTraceFailure` distinguishes a tracer-rejected step from a closed or unresolved terminal, a missing retained boundary, a wrong ocean component, and a primary-ocean trace that fails the stricter visible-grade check. For a grade failure compare the before/after segment minima, the lowest radius earlier in the trace, and the edited-facet flags. A closed terminal has a point to inspect, with no rejected segment when the tracer simply runs out of downhill exits.

The configured volume is a fraction of the cubed base radius. At radius 8, `2e-6` allows `0.001024` planet units³ and `5e-6` allows `0.00256` planet units³. A candidate volume of `0.00008198` planet units³ uses about 8% of Run A's allowance. The search uses a centreline volume estimate for pruning, measures each blended profile on the float-projected facets, and checks the combined projected volume and depth before the verification TIN can authorize a commit. `GeodesicMajorOutletBudget` logs the input fraction, converted limits, last proposed batch and committed volume.
