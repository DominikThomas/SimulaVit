## Evidence at the branch point

- **Previous user measurements.** At about 30,000 organisms and requested 100×, actual speed was about 0.6–0.7 simulated seconds per real second, with roughly 5 s per frame and 0.2 FPS. One recent mixed-ecosystem capture reported 3,125 ms/frame for lifecycle and founders, 1,073 for active movement and sensing, 507 for biological reactions including competition, and 116 for passive movement. Other user captures showed much higher relative passive costs or active costs closer to lifecycle. Population composition affects which subsystem leads.
- **Earlier reports.** The scalability assessment supplies the dependency, state ownership, and scheduling analysis. The baseline report documents the original markers and frame aggregation. The environment report records an earlier low-population capture and separate transport and thermal profiling, with its attribution limits. Treat those results as historical. [Scalability assessment](GEODESIC_SCALABILITY_ARCHITECTURE_ASSESSMENT.md) · [Baseline report](GeodesicPerformanceBaseline.md) · [Environmental CPU report](GeodesicEnvironmentalCpuOptimization.md)
- **Current source inspection.** Since the assessment, B2 added availability controls, four more dissolved-resource strategies using ordered measured transactions, and predation. Predation adds a lazily built prey index, bounded searches, and capture commits ahead of reverse lifecycle removal. Optional founders can do work at startup or at their existing positions around the lifecycle pass. The packed population, movement and reaction cadences, and separate environment drivers remain the basis for these phases. Startup now configures the performance recorder. Source shows a separable environmental read with cache hits and misses tied to position and the current thermal epoch. Gather basic and detailed captures at 1,000 and 10,000 in A and D, with C as the active-movement comparison. ## Phases and decision gates

Use paired captures with the same configuration and workload bands. Repeat comparisons three times and require changes above the observed run-to-run spread. The percentages below are initial engineering gates for selecting and stopping work. Where requested speed is already sustained, compare CPU milliseconds per step and frame tails. At a loaded 30,000-organism 1× case, an initial branch target is at least 0.95 simulated seconds per real second, at least 30 average FPS, and p95 frame time at or below 50 ms. Set the final target with the user after seeing P0 ranges.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Replay the reference step sizes and environment tick/publication order. | A different frame split can change which resources or temperatures biology sees. Stop at one work-budget change with equivalent commits, or record any semantics change as a separate proposal. |
| **P5 — Environmental simulation** | Optimize one measured transport, thermal, or abiotic chemistry stage. | Reduce CPU ms per due tick by at least 15% at the same active node count and tick workload, with a coupled gain above noise. Record the best verified 30,000-organism interactive case. | Visual growth and identity across swap-back, CPU/GPU work shifts, and retained buffers. Reorder phases when comparable measurements show that another subsystem gives the stronger first opportunity. The P0 change ends at profiling and recording.

## Implementation and parity contract

- Start with the existing packed population and one authoritative owner per state field. Prefer a reversible incremental change using data-oriented access and, where shown to pay, Burst-compatible math, Unity Jobs, or narrow reusable native buffers. Size temporary buffers to the selected kernel’s inputs and outputs. Compare managed, Burst serial, and parallel execution only for a confirmed candidate. Count data gathering, copies, scheduling, synchronization, and outputs in both latency and memory. Consider wider state migration or ECS only with measured end-to-end evidence.
- Use published environmental inputs for independent numerical calculations. Keep ordered resource demand and commits, measured transactions and stoichiometry, carbon conservation, and the current authority for each inventory. Preserve position-dependent thermal inputs and resource/light observations at their existing points in the step.
- Preserve organism identity, reverse lifecycle traversal, admission/cap bookkeeping, daughter placement, inheritance, mutation, and existing deterministic streams with matched inputs and step sequences. Preserve death recycling and predation’s single-consumption guarantee, including swap-back identity updates. Retain founder eligibility, timings, availability controls, and the saved Hydrogen → Fermentation probability of **0.02**.
- Preserve the existing integration step, per-organism workload, system cadences, environmental update/backlog behavior, measured transfer precision, and metabolic yields in every comparison. Evaluate deliberate numerical approximations under separate documented eligibility, parity, and acceptance criteria.
